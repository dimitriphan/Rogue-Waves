include features like fog?" §1-§5 answer both from the cloud. **Then it ran on his PC** the same day, at his "Run it

## 1. Answers

**The weather (PC, §6.1: W0-W3 all pass, 12 of 12).** The weather's own code (`RogueWavesMode/Code/07_Weather.cs`) has not changed since its last PC pass
except two words ("Tide" capitalised by the text audit): the mode's self-test passed 156/0 on `ecd1991` (PC round 3,
`TESTING_ROUND_3_2026-11-05.md` §2), with W0-W3 (Rising Water floods tiles at the next Tide, Quickening Current goes to
x1.25 then x1.5, Nesting Ground sends a Halberd) on three maps, and the 2026-11-04 round saw all three on a second
player's machine. Since then, outside test code, only the vote card's weather line moved (PR #103). The release, self-test and gaps builds
compile here at 0 errors (§3). But PRs #100 and #103-#117 changed much around it and none has run on the PC, so the real
test is the mode's self-test again, plus his eye on a flooded map, which the scope (`04` [Sea Weather]) leaves to him.

**The lighting: yes, a lot, with two limits (PC, §6.2: 23/0, pictures in §6.3).** Read from the game's code (kit
`docs/16` §7.1):

| Can change at run time | How | Drastic? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fog: on or off, colour, where it starts and ends (linear) | `RenderSettings`, written by mod code | Yes: from a light haze to a wall of fog a few tiles from the camera |
| Ambient light: sky, horizon and ground colours | `RenderSettings` | Yes: night, dusk, storm grey |
| The sun: colour, strength, direction | the "Sun" `Light` | Yes: a dim blue moon, a low orange sun, long shadows |
| The whole post-processing grade (the game's own Betterfog pass, colour grading) | borrowed whole from any of the 55 stock lighting groups | As far as the base game's maps differ |
| Extra lights (a lantern in the fog) | a point `Light` | Yes: drawn, a warm pool a few tiles round (§6.3) |

- **Limit 1: no grade of our own from code.** The mod compiler can't name the URP types, so a new grade (our own bloom
  or colour numbers) needs a profile built in Unity and shipped in the pack's bundle. That is possible (the base game
  makes such a volume itself for its photo mode) but needs a Unity build on the PC; not tried.
- **Limit 2: fog is distance fog, not drifting banks.** Unity's fog tints by distance from the camera. Fog that rolls
  across the ground in banks would be our own effect (sprites or particles), new art.
- **On every machine:** nothing syncs lighting, so each machine applies the look itself. Every machine already knows
  the map's weather from the synced names (`RWRejoin.MapWeather`), so a weather look needs no new network message.
- **Three facts only the PC could show, now shown (§6.3):** every unit, building and the vessel take the fog with the
  ground (the UI does not); a look darkens the ground and the units together (no baked lighting in the way); extra
  lights are drawn. **New limit seen on the PC:** a look leaves the sky as it is, so a dark look reads as dusk; only a
  borrowed grade changes the sky.

## 2. What was built (kit 1.13; compiled here, run on the PC in §6)

- **`ModKitLight`** (`TDS_Mod/ModSource/Shared/ModKitLight.cs`, also in `TDS_Vessel_Creator/kit/Shared/`): a `Look`
  (fog colour and distances, fixed or as multiples of the camera's distance to the ground; the three ambient colours;
  the sun's colour, strength and direction); `Set(look, seconds)` fades to it and holds it, writing again only when
  something (the game's own lighting call at a map's load) wrote over it; `Grade(name, sky)` borrows a stock group's
  post-processing whole, keeping the map's own sun, shadows and reflection probe; `Restore(seconds)`; `Lamp`; the stock
  groups by name. The hold drops when the map goes. Pitfall #167: never touch `Bl_Lighting_Group.Profile`.
- **The mode** calls `ModKitLight.Update()` every frame; with no look set it does nothing. No weather and no map uses a
  look: the release game is unchanged.
- **The gaps build's `light` part** (`RogueWavesMode/Code/92_GapsLight.cs`), its own launch, one calm map, a picture of
  each step:

| Check | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| L0 | The stock groups read by name (each one's numbers logged: fog, ambient, sun, sky), the camera (perspective or orthographic, its distance to the ground), and the map's own lighting is what's on screen |
| L1 | Five looks made in code, each read back from what the game draws: sea fog (`A9BCBF`), light mist, night (`0E1726`, sun x0.25), storm (`4A5258`, low sun), dusk (`E09A6B`, low orange sun) |
| L2 | The game's own lighting call, as at a map's load, can't undo a held look |
| L3 | A 4 s fade from the map's own lighting into sea fog passes between the two |
| L4 | Six stock grades borrowed (`Map_Final`, `Map_Thehunt`, `Map_Crimson`, `Map_Snobound`, `Map_Hydragen`, `Macro_World`), alone and under sea fog |
| L5 | A lamp by night, beside the vessel, in the map's scene |
| L6 | A 2 s fade back ends on the map's own lighting; each look's cost per frame |

The code review at high found ten things in the first draft, each dealt with before the PR: a relative fog with no
camera yet fogged the whole screen; a grade with no held look outlived its map; a grade took the stock map's shadow
strength and reflections; fades popped fog in and out; the hold rewrote the ambient light every frame; the test's L2
had no guard for a map with no lighting and its L5 check could not fail; the pitfall and kit rows the header cited were
missing. The tenth (each part compiles its own copy, so two parts holding looks would fight) is answered by a rule in
the header: one part holds the pack's lighting, the mode. Found after it: the last step of a fade was never written.

### 6.4 What it means for a look in play

- A fog look works as fog: what is far fades, units included. But the UI shows through, so a marker over a hidden tower
  or a foe's health bar would give away what the fog hides. No foe was on screen: foes in fog are still to see.
- A dark look alone leaves the sky bright. Night needs a grade with it (the sky follows a grade), or a look that is dark
  everywhere the camera sees no sky. Sea fog and storm don't have the problem: the fog covers the sky's edge.
- The design call in §5 is now open with pictures; it goes to the open-decisions page. Claude's lean, from the
  pictures: **(a)** a look per weather, light enough to keep play readable (light mist or storm grey under Rising Water,
  dusk under Nesting Ground), as the cheapest way to make the weather read at a glance; heavy sea fog only as **(b)**,
  a weather of its own, once foes in fog are seen.
