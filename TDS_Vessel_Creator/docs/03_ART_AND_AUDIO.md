# 03 · Art and audio

All art and sound is **generated by code** in the Unity editor (`Assets/<Name>Mod/Editor`, menu
`Tools > <Name> > Build All Assets`), so every tweak is a re-run and the output always matches the game's formats.
The template's generator (Mummer) is the starting point: keep its sheet layouts, builders and library format; redraw
the content.

| Editor file | Does |
|---|---|
| `<Name>Px.cs` | `Px` pixel canvas: `Set/Over/Draw/Stamp(rows, palette)`, `FlipX`, `Rot`, `Shift`, `Scale`, `WithOutline`, `Px.Sheet(cells, cw, ch)`, `Px.H("hex")`, `Mix` |
| `<Name>Character.cs` | the vessel and unit sprites, UI art (`MainSheet`, `Fall`, `Land`, attack/ability sheets, `MiniSheet`, `DollSheet`, `TabIcon`, `Splash`, `ReadyUp`, `EndWin/EndLose`) |
| `<Name>Lowpoly.cs` | `Lp` mesh builder (flat-shaded primitives on an 8×8 palette): `Block`, `Tube`, `Prism`, `Stem`, `Ball`, `Dome`, `Spike`, `Leaf`, `Disc`, `Flower`, `Tri`, `Quad`, `Push/Pop` transforms |
| `<Name>Models.cs` | every tower model, upgrade model, unit prop, projectile and debuff mesh |
| `<Name>Audio.cs` | synthesised sound effects (`Tone`, `Hiss`, `Ding`, envelopes) written as WAV |
| `<Name>Builder.cs` | Build All Assets, Preview Sprites, Preview Models; imports, VFX prefabs, the library prefab, manifest and localization CSV |
| `<Name>ModPrefab.cs` | Build Mod Prefab, Park Other Mods, Export Mod, Restore Parked Mods |

## 1. The vessel sprite

**Formats** (read from the game's own sheets):

| Sheet (template name) | Size | Layout | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `<Name>-Sheet` | 192×256, 32 px cells | 6 cols × 8 rows: rows 0-3 Idle (variants v0-v3 × 6 frames), rows 4-7 Move | Idle, Move, Landquick, journal portrait (frame 0) |
| `<Name>Fall-Sheet` | 384×32 | 12 frames | Falling (loading screen, `Entry.Loadingsprite`) |
| `<Name>IntroR-Sheet` | 384×32 | 12 frames | Land (match start, `Entry.Landingsprite`) |
| `<Name>Idlejustidle-Sheet` | strip | idle frames | `Entry.Idlesprite` |
| `<Name>Attack-Sheet` | 128×128 | 4 variants × 4 frames | Attack (the stock attack AI's "Attack"/"Shoot") |
| ability sheet (`<Name>Hook-Sheet`) | 192×128 | 4 variants × 6 frames | the ability's animation |

- Pixels per unit **20**, pivot **bottom-centre** (16, 0), Point filtering, no compression.
- Variants: v0 and v1 face the camera (near-identical), v2 and v3 face away (drawn, **not mirrored**).
- Animation table (`<Name>Assets.Hero`): Idle and Move loop (priority 0); Falling loops (reset, priority 2); Land
  and Landquick go to Idle (priority 2); Attack and abilities priority 1, speed ~3 and ~1.6, go to Idle. Put the
  template's footstep sound (`General_Footstep`) on the Move frame where the vessel lands its hop.
- The vessel body is about 16 px wide and 20-24 px tall inside the 32 px frame.

**House style: what all five base vessels share** (any of them can be the reference; the user's rule):
- **Chibi proportions**: the head (or head-shaped body) is half the height or more; the body is small; hands are
  small (floating or stubby) and legs short (2 px) or absent. Clear silhouette at 1× zoom.
- **No black outline.** Edges are darker or half-transparent shades of the fill (the fill colour at alpha 127 makes
  the soft rim); details inside the body use darker tones of the same colour.
- **Dark dot eyes** (`4A4949` / `333232`), a tiny mouth or none; expression through eye position and squash.
- **Palette**: soft pastel or muted main colours with one or two saturated accents (Boutman's red overalls, Fie's
  orange hair, Buster's teal collar, Smacky's blue overalls), light shading on one side.
- **One prop or signature feature** reads better than detail (Smacky's sprout, Boutman's hat and rod, Sno Sno's stick
  arms, Fie's hair, Buster's collar).
- **Idle is alive**: a small loop of 6 frames (Smacky's sprout sways, Buster wags and shifts his weight).
- Simple shapes beat texture: Mummer's felt "nap" dots looked like dirt and became a single sheen line.

**Pick the reference vessel** closest to the concept and copy its proportions, palette weight and finish:

| Base vessel | Look | Good reference for |
|---|---|---|
| Smacky | yellow square head with a sprout, small blue overalled body, round floating hands, fuzzy soft rim | square or boxy characters, simple mascots |
| Sno Sno | two round white masses, stick arms, pale blue shading on the facing side, a snow patch on the ground, hop | round or blobby characters, objects come alive (Sprigg's bulb-in-pot, Mummer's costume) |
| Boutman | blue cat in a wide fisherman's hat and red overalls, holding a rod, cheek tufts, arms out | animals in clothes, characters with a held prop |
| Fie | small human: huge orange spiky hair covering one eye, red shirt, dark trousers | human or humanoid characters, hair/hat silhouettes |
| Buster | cream dog sitting, big head and ear, teal collar, wagging tail, poses change a lot | animals, energetic characters with big pose changes |

Reference images (game art, keep them local, don't ship them): menu art of all five (icon, idle strip, landing and
loading strips, splash, boat mini) and full sheets of Sno Sno and Buster are in
`C:\Users\Dimitri\TDS_Mod\ModSource\Sprigg\Research\dump`. To dump the full sheets of any other vessel, port
`examples/code/Sprigg_97_ArtDump.cs` into your mod under a `<NAME>_ARTDUMP` define, put that vessel's id in its
`foreach (string want in new[] { ... })` list, export, launch, and decode the dump lines (`docs/08`).

Feedback on past vessels (apply when the concept is similar): Sprigg (Sno Sno-like) got a dopey face with the eyes wide
apart and one a pixel lower; Argo's hands had to touch the body and its build be bulky "like Nautilus"; Mummer's
peeking arm had to be drawn in front of the costume.

## 3. Units

16 px cells, 4 rows (v0-v3), 10 columns: Move in 0-5 (Idle = column 0), Attack in 6-9 (`<Name>Assets.Puppet`).
Same style as the vessel, smaller (the miner, knight and dog are 6-10 px tall). Variants per costume or skin get
their own sheet.

## 4. Towers: low-poly sleek, never voxel

The user's rule, with no exceptions: towers look like **Sno Sno's or Buster's** (smooth bevelled solids, cylinders,
domes, clean silhouettes, flat palette shading), **never voxel**, whichever vessel the sprites follow. Voxels only look
good when handmade; generated voxel towers (Sprigg 1.1) looked messy.

- Built with `Lp`: a few clean primitives per model, flat-shaded, coloured from one **8×8 point-filtered palette**
  texture (64 swatches). 40-800 triangles per model.
- **Coordinates**: origin at the tile centre, ground at y = -0.5 (primitives are written with y measured up from the
  ground), the front faces **-z** (toward the camera).
- **Size**: footprint inside the tile (|x|, |z| ≤ ~0.42); height 0.8-1.45 for normal towers. Measured shipped
  heights: Sno Sno 0.75-1.15, Buster 0.85 and 1.9, Smacky 1.0-2.05. Sprigg's 0.6 towers looked small.
- **Readable from the game camera** (high, looking down and forward): put signature details on the top and front
  faces. Mummer's quilt and tapestry first sat on the far side and were invisible in game. Thin parts need
  ~0.05 minimum thickness (the first Marionette Cross was too spindly).
- **Upgrades** keep the base silhouette and add one clear feature per upgrade (a finial, a banner, a second turret).
- **Prefab layout**: `<Model>` / `Vis` / `<Model>_Export` (static mesh). Moving parts go under `Vis/<Part>` at their
  pivot and the client animates them, or at `<Model>/Pivot` for the game's native turret script (§4a). Put a **disabled** MeshRenderer of the whole model on `Vis` so the journal's 3D
  view frames the whole tower, as the game's Mining Hut and Wall do.
- **Material**: the generator uses a preview material; at run time `<Name>Assets.ApplyTowerMaterial` copies the
  game's own tower material (`Shader Graphs/General Tower`, from `Tower_Smackyarcher`) and sets its `_MainTex` to the
  palette, so mod towers are lit like the rest.
- Skins add variants (`Additionalprefabs`, e.g. the Stage's costume hangers).

### 4a. Towers that move: aiming, spinning, recoil, idle motion, states

Decide from context. A tower that **shoots at a target** should turn toward it (a fixed cannon firing sideways looks
broken). A tower that **works** (drill, loom, pump) should show it while it works. A tower that does neither stays still
or has a gentle idle. A tower whose **mode changes** shows the mode with a model or skin, not a motion.

How the game draws a tower: each machine instantiates the visual prefab (`Entry.Visprefab`) as
`Entity_Master_Client.Currentvis`, parented under the entity's own `Vis` child (`Visobj`). **It is destroyed and
re-instantiated whenever the visual changes** (an upgrade's `Ent_Visual`, a skin), so never cache a part's Transform
for long: look it up again when it's null. Never move or rotate the entity root or `Visobj` (the game moves them).
All motion is client-side and cosmetic; the server only sends the events.

| Motion | How | Verified |
|---|---|---|
| **Aim at each target** (turret) | **Native**: add the game's visual script `Entity_Vis_Tower_Boutmanboat_Cannon` to the tower's `Visualscripts` (name carrier `<Name>Util.Script(...)`); give the model a part named **`Pivot`** as a **direct child of the prefab root** (it turns flat, yaw only) and optionally `Pivot/Gun` (also pitches). On each shot the server sends `master.Radio.Vis_ClientRpc("Shoot", targetPos - muzzlePos, 0f)`: the Position is a **direction**. The script does `Pivot.LookAt(Pivot.position + flat(dir))` and `Gun.LookAt(Gun.position + dir)`, so the barrel must point along the part's local **+z**. This is how the Boutboat's and Sno Cannon's guns aim (don't use `Entity_Vis_Tower_Snosnocannon`: it also swaps Sno Sno's Snoble sprites). | read from the game's code; not yet used by a mod |
| **Aim, custom** | Argo 1.0: the server sends its own Vis (`"Argo_Scatter"`, Position = target); the client finds the part (`"Turret"`, anywhere under the entity) and sets `turret.rotation = Quaternion.LookRotation(-flatDir, Vector3.up)` (Argo's barrels point local **-z**, the camera side); a 0.25 s recoil coroutine kicks the part back along the shot and returns it. `examples/code/Argo_10_Client.cs` (`Aim`, `Recoil`, `Part`). Use this when you also want muzzle effects, recoil or several parts. | in game (Argo 1.0, 64/64) |
| **Spin while working** | The server's work event (`"Argo_Mine"`) makes the client spin a `"Spinner"` part for N seconds: a dictionary part → until-time, rotated each frame with `Rotate(0, 540·dt, 0, Space.Self)` (`Argo_10_Client.cs`, `SpinFor`/`Spin`). | in game (Argo 1.0) |
| **Idle motion** (a wheel turning, a flag waving, a bob) | Client loop: every 0.25 s collect the vessel's towers from `Game_Entity.Instance.Entityclient` (by being name), each frame rotate/offset their named part a little; re-find parts that became null. Keep it slow and subtle; base towers are mostly still. Argo 2.0's Deep Water: the water is a `Swell` part (the frame stays still) raised ±0.012 over 3.2 s, phased by tile so neighbouring pools roll (`ArgoClient.Bob`, `ArgoModels.Swell`). | in game (Argo 2.0 round 6: the self-test measured the bob) |
| **A held or switched state** (open/closed, loaded, a costume shown) | A skin (`<Name>Util.Skin(master, "Open")` + `Visualproperties.Skinoverrides`) or the upgrade's model: synced to every player and correct for anyone who joins later, unlike a client-side rotation. | in game (Mummer's Stage costume hangers) |
| **Facing chosen by the player** (directional traps, walls) | The building's placement orientation (`Builddata.Orientation`, `Link_Spawn.Orientation`, `Entity_Master.Extrapositiondata`); Buster's pad turns its visual in `Entity_Vis_Trap_Busterpad.Movevis` with `Helper_Gridtools.Rotateorientation`. Read the orientation on the server to apply direction-dependent effects. | read from the game's code |
| **Sprite towers / units facing** | The game's sprite towers flip and turn sprites toward targets with `Helper_Entityvis.Rotatesprite` (`Entity_Vis_Animator_Tower_Smackyarcher`). Units use `Entity_Vis_Animator_Mob`, which faces their movement. | game code |

**Building moving parts in the generator**: the template's builder (Mummer) makes one static mesh per model. Argo's
does parts: a `ModelDef` has `Part`, `PartName` and `PartPivot` (tower space, y up from the ground); `Assemble` writes
`<Model>/Vis/<Model>_Export` for the static body and `<Model>/Vis/<PartName>` at the pivot holding the part's mesh,
built around the pivot so it rotates in place (`examples/code/Argo_Editor_ArgoBuilder.cs`, `Assemble`;
`Argo_Editor_ArgoModels.cs`, `ScatterTurret`). For the **native** turret script, put the part at `<Model>/Pivot`
instead (a direct child of the root), with the barrel along +z. Either way:

- the whole-model disabled renderer on `Vis` (body + parts combined) keeps the journal's 3D view framing the tower;
- the part's swing must stay inside the tile (a barrel that sticks out 0.45 overlaps the next tile when it turns);
- every upgrade model that has the turret needs the same part name and pivot, or aiming stops after that upgrade;
- a bespoke base-game tower AI that you strip (e.g. Smacky's archer AI) took its aiming with it: re-add aiming with
  one of the two methods above.

## 5. Effects

Particle prefabs built from `VfxSpec` entries in the builder (mesh and/or particles, pixel textures `Px_Star`,
`Px_Square`, `Px_Tri`, `Px_Fluff`, colours, rate/burst, size, lifetime, radius, height).

- **World effects** `World_<Name>_<X>`: played by the client (`<Name>Assets.Effect`) when the server sends a Vis event.
- **Debuff looks** `Debuff_<Name><x>`: registered in the mod's `Vfx` list and attached to the target by the game
  (`docs/05` §8). They sit under the target's `Vis` child, whose origin is the target's **feet**; an overhead marker
  needs ~1.4-1.7 units of height to clear tall enemy sprites. Keep them small, bold and loopable.
- Ground effects go at entity position − 0.5 (`Entityposition.y` is ground + 0.5).
- **Unity's Hemisphere particle shape faces +Z**: rotate it (the builder's `HemisphereUp()` probes and fixes it), or
  ground bursts spray sideways.
- Area effects scale to the real radius at run time (`ParticleSystemScalingMode.Shape`, or scale the transform).

## 6. Sound

Synthesised (`<Name>Audio.cs`): 44.1 kHz, 16-bit, mono, normalised. The game's sound table is private, so the mod
plays its own `AudioSource`s (`<Name>Assets.Play`): spatial blend 1, linear rolloff, the game's `Mindist`/`Maxdist`,
pitch ±6 %, routed to the **mixer group of the game's "Upgrade" sound** (`General_Main`) so the volume sliders apply.

**Levels**: clip rms × volume measured on the game's own sounds: tower shots ~0.004, hits ~0.0015, Upgrade ~0.013,
vessel landings ~0.015. Set each clip's volume in `<Name>Data.SoundVolume` (Mummer: 0.008 footstep - 0.1 landing).
Mod sounds play on top of the game's own (placement, death, landing), so keep them accents. Sprigg's first sounds were
~20× too loud.

Typical set (15-20 clips): attack, ability, ability hit, each building's action, unit spawn and death, resource gained,
mode switch, landing, death, footstep, projectile.

## 7. The library prefab

`Assets/<Name>Mod/Prefabs/<Name>Assets_Library.prefab`, built by the builder, nested in the mod prefab:

```
<Name>Assets_Library
  Sprites/<SheetName>   SpriteRenderer (sprite.texture = the whole sheet)
  Models/<ModelName>    nested model prefab
  VFX/<EffectName>      nested effect prefab
  Audio/<ClipName>      AudioSource with the clip
```

Built-in components only (the runtime finds things by child name). **Every holder is saved inactive**: the game
instantiates the mod prefab at startup, and an active Sprites holder once drew Sprigg's sheets on the startup screen.
`Transform.Find` and `GetComponent` still reach inactive children.

## 8. Working loop

1. `Tools > <Name> > Preview Sprites` → `ModSource/<Name>/Previews/wip/*.png` (4× on a checker); `Preview Models` →
   models rendered at the game's camera angle in a grid. Look at them (Read the PNGs) before building.
2. `Build All Assets` once the previews are right. **Wait until Unity has recompiled your editor edits first**
   (`Library/ScriptAssemblies/Assembly-CSharp-Editor.dll` newer than the edited .cs), or the old builder runs.
3. In-game captures from the self-test (`docs/08`): the boat, the buildings together, every effect on a real foe.
4. Compare side by side with shipped vessels (the boat capture shows them all); fix what stands out.

If the user supplied a concept (an `index.html` with sprite sheets and a `manifest.json`), treat its art as the design:
keep the silhouette, colours and signature features, and redraw it in the generator at the game's formats and style.
